When Cinema Looks Back

Floor plan, M3301 Gallery, CMC Level 3. To walk through the room in 3D, open this file in a web browser (on a computer, or on Android choose "Open with Chrome").

  • 1.1
    FILM
    Tacita Dean (British, b. 1965), 2011
    Dean made FILM as a tribute to analogue celluloid, at a time when cinemas were switching to digital. The tall image stands like a monolith, filled with colours and textures that only film can make. It returns us to the most familiar position in cinema: standing before an image and simply watching. This is where the journey begins (Marian Goodman Gallery, n.d.).
  • 1.2
    Looking Back: Fourth-Wall Clips
    Various directors (film and television), 1960–2019
    In each clip, a character turns to the camera and looks straight at us. The moment is brief, but it breaks the rule that the audience watches unseen. Here, cinema looks back for the first time.
  • 1.3
    Performer/Audience/Mirror
    Dan Graham (American, 1942–2022), 1975
    Graham stands before a seated audience with a mirrored wall behind him. He describes the audience aloud, then turns to the mirror and describes their reflections. The people who came to watch become the subject of the work. The fourth wall is not only broken but turned into a mirror (M+, n.d.).
  • 2.1
    Line Describing a Cone
    Anthony McCall (British, b. 1946), 1973
    A single white dot slowly draws a circle on the wall for over 30 minutes. In the hazy room, the projector beam becomes a visible cone of light. Visitors can stand inside it and break it with their hands. The film is no longer only on the wall; it is the light around the viewer's body.
  • 3.1
    Forest of Us
    Es Devlin (British, b. 1971), 2021
    The work opens with a film that links the branches of human lungs to the branches of trees. The film then gives way to a mirror maze, where visitors' reflections split and merge with those of strangers (Devlin, 2018). Inspired by the moving walls of The Maze Runner, we place it at the centre of the gallery, where watching turns into walking through the image.
  • 3.2
    Floor Film
    Tony Hill (British, b. 1946), 1975
    The screen is the floor of a small room, and visitors stand on it to watch. The film shows people filmed from below, as if through glass, so their feet seem to meet the visitors' feet. Standing on the image, the viewer becomes part of it (Light Cone, 2020).
  • 4.1
    In the Scene
    Curatorial installation (after The Truman Show, Peter Weir, 1998), 2026
    A screen plays a short clip from The Truman Show, then goes dark and becomes a mirror. It reflects each visitor together with the wall behind them, painted as the film's sky set. For a moment, the visitor stands inside the film. Then, like Truman, they step out of the set and back into the real world.
